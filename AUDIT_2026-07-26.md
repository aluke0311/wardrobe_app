# Wardrobe App — Comprehensive Audit
**2026-07-26 · audited against the philosophy approved the same day, not against CLAUDE.md.**
Where the two conflict, the philosophy wins and CLAUDE.md is what's wrong.

Everything marked **Verified** was traced in the actual source, not inferred from docs.

---

## The one-paragraph version

The app is in much better structural shape than it feels. There are no zombie
references from the r19 deletion, the version is in lockstep across all 23 places,
the selftest is real and green at 127, and the analytical features you like are
genuinely well-built. **The problem is almost entirely at two seams: the way in,
and the way things are drawn.** The single best feature in the app has no entry
point on the screen you open every morning, and forty-five CSS rules pay for two
kinds of visual separation while only one of them is doing any work. Those two
findings account for most of "hard to access" and most of "clunky, ugly,
overwhelming." Neither requires new features to fix.

# CRITICAL

## C1. The suggester has no entry point on Home, and the one path in is labelled as a logging action

**Why it matters.** This is the whole of your first complaint, and it's worse than
you described. On a normal (non-trip) day, Home renders:

```
dash · tiles · todayPlanRows · cta · tomorrowCard · attention · onThisDay
```

None of those contains a suggest action. `data-td-suggest` (js/06-home.js:733)
lives inside `tripDashHtml` — **trip mode only**. `data-tm-refine`
(js/06-home.js:882) is inside the Tomorrow card and only appears when a *planned*
entry can't be filled. So the only route to today's suggestions is:

> Home → **"Log today's wear"** → wear-again chooser → **✨ Suggest new** → sheet

Three taps behind a button that says *log*. You told me your mornings are either
"no idea, staring at the closet" or "rough idea, want a better version." In
neither of those states are you logging anything, so you would never reach for
that button. The feature you love most is behind the one label that describes a
thing you aren't doing yet.

**And it gets worse after you log.** `hasWearToday` swaps the CTA for a
`logged-row` whose handler (js/06-home.js:1158) only opens the calendar day view.
So once you've logged anything today, **the path to the suggester disappears from
Home entirely** — you're down to Calendar → day → footer `↻ Wear again` → ✨. You
specifically said you often change one piece later in the day. That's exactly when
the feature vanishes.

**Third problem on the same path:** the CTA calls `switchTab("calendar")` *before*
opening the chooser (js/06-home.js:1152). Asking "what should I wear" silently
moves you to the Calendar tab, and closing the sheet strands you there instead of
back on Home.

**Where.** `js/06-home.js:965–980, 1149–1163` · `js/14-calendar.js:920–930`

**Fix.** A permanent, unconditional **"What should I wear?"** action on Home that
opens the suggester directly — present whether or not you've logged, never behind
the chooser, no tab change. The wear-again chooser stays exactly where it is for
the logging flow; it just stops being the only door. Logging and deciding are two
different intentions and should not share an entry point.

**Confidence: High.** Verified in source.

## C2. `↻` means three different things, and two of them are opposites

**Why it matters.** Your words: "the little shuffle button means two different
things in two different places." Confirmed, and it's three:

| Where | Glyph | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar day footer (`js/14-calendar.js:318`) | `↻ Wear again` | show me **outfits I already own** |
| Suggester (`js/12-looks.js:1711`) | `↻ Reshuffle outfit` | **generate a new** combination |
| Suggester per-piece (`js/12-looks.js:1692`) | `↻ <slot>` | swap **one piece** |
| Tomorrow card (`js/06-home.js:890`) | `↻` | re-roll a **new** combination |

Meanwhile `✨` is used consistently and correctly for "make something new" in six
places. So you have a coherent icon (`✨`) *and* an incoherent one (`↻`) doing
overlapping work, which is the worst of both.

This directly violates **recognition, never recall** — the icon requires you to
remember which screen you're on to know what it does.

**Fix.** One meaning per glyph, app-wide:
- `✨` = generate something new (suggester, reshuffle, re-roll, per-piece swap)
- `↻` = **retired entirely**, or reserved for literal refresh (the weather ↻)
- `↺` or a plain label = "wear again" / from history

The calendar footer button becomes **"Wear again"** with no glyph or a history
glyph. It's a different verb from everything else and shouldn't borrow an icon.

**Confidence: High.** Verified in source.

## C3. Tapping a calendar day opens a menu instead of the day — and corrupts the scroll stack doing it

**Why it matters.** You asked for this directly. Confirmed: `[data-calday]`
(js/14-calendar.js:186) calls `openCalDaySheet`, which renders a sheet with up to
three buttons — *View day*, *+ Add clothing*, *+ Add look*. You always want the
first one. On a future date with nothing logged the sheet contains **only the text
"Nothing logged yet"** — a modal whose entire content is an apology.

**The hidden bug.** That same line calls `navDeeper('calendar')` when the *sheet*
opens, not when you navigate. Two consequences:

1. `navDeeper` calls `scrollToTop()`, so the month grid silently scrolls to the top
   behind the sheet. Cancel, and you've lost your place in the month.
2. **Cancel never pops.** Only `#calDayBack` calls `navShallower`
   (js/14-calendar.js:327). Every cancelled day-tap leaves an orphan entry on
   `_navScroll.calendar`, which grows unbounded for the session and desynchronises
   the offsets — so a later back-from-day restores a scroll position captured at
   an unrelated moment.

**Fix.** `[data-calday]` opens the day directly. Move `navDeeper('calendar')` into
that navigation. Add-clothing and add-look already exist in the day footer, which
is where they belong. `openCalDaySheet` gets deleted — it's a menu that exists to
choose between one thing you always want and two things already available one
screen deeper.

**Confidence: High.** Verified in source.

## C4. The visual system pays for two separations and only uses one — this is the "ugly"

**Why it matters.** Your exact words: *"outlines and background colors don't work
together, look ugly."* You're right, and it's measurable.

**45 rules** paint both a `background` and a `border`. The tokens:

```
--bg:      #f8f4ee     (page)
--surface: #fffdfb     (cards)
--line:    #e5dbcb     (borders)
```

`--bg` and `--surface` differ by roughly **1.5% luminance**. That fill is
invisible — it cannot separate a card from the page. So `--line` is doing 100% of
the separation work, and every card is nonetheless carrying a fill it doesn't
benefit from. The result is a page of hairline-outlined boxes on paper, all at
**identical visual weight**, with no hierarchy between "this is the main thing" and
"this is a footnote."

That is precisely what "too many pieces" and "overwhelming" feel like. It isn't
the number of features — it's that forty features are all drawn as the same box.

**Fix — the coherence pass, in one rule.** Pick one mode and commit:

- **Outline mode:** keep `--line`, drop the fill (`background: transparent`) from
  every card. Instant coherence, zero risk, one find-and-replace.
- **Fill mode (better):** push `--surface` far enough from `--bg` to actually
  read as a raised plane, then **drop the border**. Warmer, quieter, more
  editorial — and it lets you reintroduce the border as a *meaningful* signal
  (selected, active, needs attention) instead of decoration.

I'd do fill mode, and I'd hold one rule afterward: **a border is a state, never a
container.** That single sentence in CLAUDE.md prevents this from ever coming back.

Then a three-tier weight scale so pages have a shape: one primary block per screen,
secondary blocks quieter, footnotes with no container at all.

**Confidence: High.** Verified by measurement.

# HIGH

## H1. `.otd-row` styles a memory and an action identically

**Where.** `js/14-calendar.js:55` ("On this day" — last year's outfit, a memory) and
`js/14-calendar.js:306` ("📅 Planned: X · **Wear it ✓**" — an action that writes to
the database). Same class, same look.

**Why it matters.** You said "On this day feels in the way." The reason is sharper
than crowding: a nostalgic row and a commit-a-wear button are visually
indistinguishable, so your eye can't triage the day view. And in the day-view
render order, the memory sits **between** your actual wears and your plan
(`cardsHtml → create-look → onThisDay → planned → footer`), which is backwards on
every axis — recency, actionability, and relevance.

**Fix.** Two separate treatments: **actions** keep the button look; **memories**
become a quiet, borderless, italic line — no box at all. Then move "On this day"
**below the footer**, at the very bottom of the day. It's a reward for scrolling,
not an item in your workflow. (Home already gets this right — the comment at
js/06-home.js:1037 explicitly calls it "a delight row, not an attention row." The
day view didn't inherit that thinking.)

**Confidence: High.**

## H2. kv writes are whole-blob overwrites with no conflict handling

**Where.** `kvSet(key, value)` — optimistic upsert with
`Prefer: resolution=merge-duplicates`, one row per key, value is the entire JSON
object. 11 live keys: `wxlog`, `dayplan`, `wherelog`, `taxonomy`, `milestones`,
`tmpick`, `wxaudit_ok`, `beststreak`, plus backfill/archive markers.

**Why it matters.** You use this on a phone and a desktop. Open both, and the
second write wins **entirely** — not per-field, the whole blob. A plan made on the
phone this morning is silently erased by a desktop tab that loaded before it. There
is no version, no timestamp, no merge. The snapshot cache makes it likelier: a
stale tab hydrates from a 7-day-old snapshot and can write it straight back.

This is the single most likely way you lose real data, and it will fail silently.

**Fix.** Cheap version: store `{v: <epoch>, data: …}` and refuse a write whose
loaded `v` is older than what's on the server — re-fetch and retry instead. It's
maybe twenty lines and it converts silent loss into a correct write.

**Confidence: High** on the mechanism. **Medium** on how often you'd actually hit
it — but the cost when you do is unrecoverable.

## H3. `wxlog` is one JSON blob that only grows

**Where.** `WXLOG_DAYS = 1200`, `kvSet("wxlog")` × 4 sites.

**Why it matters.** ~1,200 day-entries in a single row, rewritten in full on every
daily log and every backfill, and carried in the snapshot. Fine today (~70–100KB).
In five years of daily use with the window widened again, it's the thing that makes
boot slow, and it's the largest blob exposed to the H2 clobber. "Used every day for
decades" is your stated frame — this is the field that doesn't survive it.

**Fix.** Not urgent, but decide now: either hold the 1,200-day cap as a real
invariant (with a test pinning it), or move `wxlog` to its own table keyed by date.
The second is a migration you'd rather do at 1,200 rows than at 10,000.

**Confidence: High.**

## H4. The rack will starve the hard formality filter unless it's stratified

**Why it matters.** Pre-empting a bug in unbuilt work, because it's the same bug you
already reported once. `suggestOutfits` applies `targetLevel` as a **hard** filter
(js/12-looks.js:1030) — deliberately, after the 2026-07-19 capsule bug where a
"Dressed Up" ask silently fell back to casual. Correct decision. But it means a
pool that doesn't cover a level returns **zero results**.

A rack chosen by "what you've reached for lately" will be overwhelmingly levels 2–3.
The first time you ask it for a concert or church, you get an empty sheet — and it
will look like the app is broken, not like the rack is thin.

**Fix.** Formality quotas are non-negotiable in the rack build, derived from the
levels your wear history actually contains. Plus: when the rack starves, the
existing starvation note should say *"nothing in your rack at this level — widen to
the whole closet?"* with the widen as a button. Never an empty sheet.

**Confidence: High.**

## H5. Season is derived three different ways and consumers pick different ones

**Where.** `itemSeasonSet` (explicit wins, else derived) · `derivedSeasonSet`
(ignores explicit entirely) · `effectiveSeasonOf` (weather-based) ·
`inSeasonWx` (temperature profile ± `WXA_RESCUE_MARGIN`) · plus `matchesSeason` for
filtering and a plain `inSeason` for suggestions where unknown = eligible.

**Why it matters.** Six related concepts, each correct in isolation, with genuinely
different semantics for "unknown" — filters treat unknown as *no match*, suggestions
treat it as *all-season eligible*. Nothing is broken today. But this is the area a
future you will misread at a glance, and the "unknown" divergence is exactly the
kind of thing that produces a bug you can't reproduce.

**Fix.** Don't refactor. Write a five-line comment block at the top of the season
section stating which function is authoritative for which question and what unknown
means in each. This is a documentation fix for a comprehension problem.

**Confidence: Medium** — no live bug found; flagged as maintainability.

## M2. Trip mode and the rack need an explicit precedence rule, written before either touches the other

Trip mode already overrides the pool everywhere (`_pickTripScope`, `builderPool`,
`openSuggestSheet` defaults). The rack will also override the pool. Two mechanisms
that both mean "the pool is smaller now" will collide, and the failure mode is a
suggester scoped to the *intersection* of your suitcase and your rack — which could
easily be four items.

**Fix.** Decide now and write it in CLAUDE.md: **during a trip, the suitcase IS the
rack.** They never compose. The rack chip in the suggester reads "Suitcase · 22"
and widening goes to the whole closet, skipping the rack entirely.

**Confidence: High** that this needs deciding. It's cheap now and expensive later.

# CONCEPTUAL COMPRESSION

You asked for this specifically, and it's where the biggest structural win is.

## The four virtual closets

**Hamper**, **Worn tray**, **Mending**, and **capsule/trip scope** are four separate
mechanisms with four separate full-page views, four `closetBack` branches, four
`siblingItems` branches, and four `switchTab` branches — implementing one idea:
*a named subset of the closet.*

Add the rack and it's five. **Don't.**

**The unification.** One concept — call it a **shelf** — with one renderer, one
scope banner, one back behaviour, one set of siblings. The differences between them
collapse to a predicate and a label:

| Shelf | Predicate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hamper | `isDirty` | derived |
| Worn | `isWornNotDirty` | derived |
| Rack | rack membership | derived + nudged |
| Suitcase | `capsuleItems(id)` | stored |
| Capsule | `capsuleItems(id)` | stored |

That removes roughly three parallel view implementations, gives the rack a home
that costs zero new mental model, and — most relevant to your actual complaint —
makes the closet root **one list of shelves** instead of a pile of differently-shaped
rows. This is "compress, don't delete" applied at the architecture level.

**Confidence: High** that the concepts are the same. **Medium** on effort — this is
a real refactor, not an afternoon. But it's the one refactor that pays for itself
in every future feature, and you said you intend to keep building for years.

## Plans, outfits, and wears are three states of one thing

A plan is an intention, an outfit is a saved arrangement, a wear is a fact. They're
already related by ad-hoc conversion functions (`planWoreIt`, `wearPlannedEntry`,
`tripPlanSync`, `syncWearsToLook`, `saveComboAsOutfit`) — five different pieces of
code moving between three states of the same object.

I'm flagging this but **not** recommending it. The unification is real and elegant
on paper, and it would touch the wears table, which holds 4,000 irreplaceable rows.
Not worth it. Note it in CLAUDE.md as a known conceptual redundancy so nobody
"discovers" it later and starts a migration.

**Confidence: High** on the observation. **Recommendation: don't act.**

## Report Cards, Smart Lists, and Closet Review already share an engine

`buildItemPerf` genuinely backs all three. This one's already done right — I looked
for the duplication and it isn't there. Worth saying, because it's the pattern the
shelf unification should copy.

# BRILLIANT OPPORTUNITIES

Only things that are derive-first, zero-maintenance, and consistent with "mirror
first, stylist second."

## B1. The variation you actually asked for

You said: *"I often will change just one piece of an outfit in a day — maybe a
feature to create an outfit that is a variation of another one?"*

This is better than a feature request; it's an observation about how you dress. And
the machinery is **already built**: `swapSuggestionPiece` swaps one slot while
holding everything else, and `_sugg.locked` pins the rest. The missing piece is
just an entry point — **"Vary this"** on any saved look, which opens the suggester
with every piece locked except one.

It also fixes a data problem you don't know you have. Right now, changing one piece
either creates a near-duplicate look or gets logged as loose items. Neither records
the truth: *this was Tuesday's outfit with different shoes.* Variation-as-a-verb
captures that, and it makes `buildFormulas` dramatically better, because variations
of a shape are exactly the evidence a formula is built from.

Cheap, uses existing code, and it's the thing you asked for unprompted — which
usually means it's real.

## B2. Make the cold 20% of the rack visible as a feature

The safety mechanism that stops the rack calcifying is also the best "how did it
know" moment available. A quiet line on the rack screen: *"Three pieces you haven't
reached for since March."* Not a nudge, not a nag — a fact, on a screen you chose to
open. It is simultaneously the thing that keeps the mirror honest and the thing that
makes it feel alive.

## B3. "Worn like" is the most interesting sentence in the app and it's buried

`derivedSeasonSet` compares what you *said* about a piece against what the evidence
says, and currently that only surfaces as a small line under the Season row and a
Closet Review card.

The general form is far more interesting than the season case: **the app knows
things about your wardrobe that disagree with what you told it.** You tagged it
formal, you wear it casually. You bought it for work, it's a weekend piece. You call
it a summer dress, you wear it in October.

That's not a recommendation engine — it's the mirror being honest, which is the
stated identity of the app. Given you open Stats daily *to look*, a single small
section — **"Things you might be wrong about"** — is the highest-value stats
addition available, and every input already exists.

## B4. Two answers to "why is my closet like this"

Because you look at Stats to look, not to learn, the best additions are the ones
that reward looking. Both of these are pure derivations over existing data:

- **The shape of your year** — which formality levels you actually lived at, month
  by month. Not a chart of what you own; a chart of the life you actually dressed
  for. Pairs naturally with Year in Pixels, which already computes the input.
- **First and last** — for any piece, the first day you wore it and the most recent,
  as a sentence. `wears` already has both. It's one line of derivation and it's the
  most human thing the data can say.

# WHAT I'D REFUSE TO MERGE, IF I'D INHERITED THIS

Short list, in the spirit of the brutal review you asked for.

1. **`openCalDaySheet`.** A modal to choose between one thing you always want and
   two things already available one screen deeper. It also breaks the scroll stack.
   Delete.
2. **`navDeeper` called on sheet-open.** A navigation primitive fired by a
   non-navigation event, with no matching pop on the cancel path. That's how scroll
   stacks rot.
3. **`--surface` at 1.5% off `--bg`.** A token that costs 45 rules and does nothing.
   Either make it real or remove it.
4. **`↻` as a general-purpose glyph.** Three meanings, two of them opposites.

And what I'd defend against anyone who wanted to cut it: **the analytical surfaces.**
An outside reviewer would look at Report Cards, Palette, Year in Pixels, Closet vs
Life and What's Missing and call it feature bloat in a wardrobe app. They'd be
wrong. You open Stats daily and kept 11 of 12. Those aren't decoration on a utility
— the utility is what makes them possible.

Worth recording, because an audit that only lists problems misrepresents the codebase.

- **The r19 deletion was clean.** All nine deleted symbols return zero hits across
  `js/` and `index.html`. Deleting a feature properly is rare; this was done right.
- **Version lockstep holds.** `APP_VERSION`, the meta tag, and all 21 `?v=` query
  strings agree at `2026-07-25 r22`.
- **The selftest is real.** 127 cases, and the count *dropped* from 136 when r19

  is a healthy sign, not a regression.

# RECOMMENDED ORDER

You chose a coherence pass over new features. This is that pass, sequenced so each
step makes the next one easier.

**1 — The way in.** C1 (Home suggest entry), C2 (icon meanings), C3 (day tap +
scroll stack). Small, high-relief, fixes the complaint you led with.

**2 — The way it's drawn.** C4 (pick fill or outline, commit, add the three-tier
weight scale), H1 (memories vs actions). This is where "ugly" and "overwhelming"
actually get resolved.

**3 — Safety.** H2 (kv write conflicts). Boring, invisible, and the only thing here
that can lose real data.

**4 — Then the rack**, with H4's formality quotas and M2's trip precedence built in
from the first commit — ideally on the unified shelf from the compression section,
so it costs one concept instead of five.

**5 — B1 ("Vary this")**, because it's nearly free, you asked for it, and it makes
Formulas better as a side effect.

M4 (finish deleting Mending) can ride along with any of these.
